# Unslop Design (v1.5)

A foundation skill for front-end design and web page builds. Its goal is output that looks designed by a specific studio for a specific subject, with no recognizable AI design patterns.

## What this skill is

Unslop Design is a foundation, not a finished design system. It sets the process and the hard rules. It does not decide the look of your project. The subject, concept, palette, fonts, layout and hero element come from the prompt you give it.

Think of it as the scaffolding: the steps every design goes through, plus the guardrails that keep the result from looking generic.

## What the foundation provides

- **Concept phase.** List real-world moments from the subject, write five metaphor-based concepts, score them, and prototype the risky parts before building the full page.
- **Ban list.** Hard rules against common AI patterns: extreme rounding, gradients, italics, monospace type, glossy orbs, generic hero-plus-three-cards layouts, and similar.
- **Rejection protocol.** Force past the first obvious choices for fonts, layouts, visual paradigm and palette.
- **Detail system.** Tokens for concentric radius, one motion language, and hidden symmetry.
- **Icons and images.** Icons drawn from simple geometry and checked twice. Images only when they show real content.
- **UI/UX states.** Every control gets four states: idle, working, success and failure.
- **Accessibility.** WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: contrast, keyboard paths, live regions, reduced motion, and no autoplaying sound.
- **Hero element.** One detailed, interactive element that comes from the subject and is keyboard-accessible.
- **Multi-page rules.** Shared shell, current-page markers, and one useful tool per inner page.
- **Minimal effects.** Sound and particle effects only confirm actions, and both can be turned off.
- **User psychology walkthrough.** First five seconds, intended feeling, load, click count, and freshness.
- **Self-test scorecard.** A pass/fail table to confirm each rule was followed.

## What you supply in your prompt

- The subject and audience (for example, a hotpot shop or a portfolio)
- The page or pages to build
- Any brand constraints: colors, fonts, logos, or content you already have
- Functional requirements: forms, booking, live data, or other behavior

The skill then turns that into a concept, a palette, a layout and a hero.

## How to use it

1. Give your subject and the pages you need.
2. Let the skill run the concept and rejection phases. Check the five concepts and the chosen one.
3. Review the prototype of the hero before the full build.
4. Read the scorecard at the end. Any failed rule should be explained or fixed.

## Notes

- Fonts and layouts are chosen to avoid the usual defaults, so they may not match what you expected. Ask for a specific font or layout in your prompt if you need one.
- If you want a brand look kept exactly, say so in the prompt. The skill's freshness rules otherwise push toward a new look.
